How the value is built

The model is fit at run time from the snapshot's own token-bearing protocols. No protocol name is ever special-cased: the same function scores every row.

  1. The curve. On the 128 comps, ln(market cap) is regressed on ln(TVL): ln(mcap) = . A protocol twice as big is worth about 1.4× as much, not twice — the market pays less per dollar of TVL as protocols grow.
  2. The group. Near-duplicate categories are pooled (bridges together, staking together, yield together, lending together, dexes together). Each group's offset is the shrunk median of its curve residuals, pulled toward the global median by its size. Bridge protocols trade rich, staking protocols trade cheap; the board prices that in instead of pretending every category is one market.
  3. The second opinion. A median (L1) regression on TVL, category, seven-day growth, log age, chain count and chain flags reads what the curve cannot see: growth, age and chains. Ridge penalty 10, fit by iteratively reweighted least squares.
  4. The blend. Implied value = 0.9 × grouped curve + 0.1 × second opinion, exponentiated. The curve carries the ranking; the second opinion corrects it where growth, age or chains say otherwise. The weight was chosen by a sweep, not by taste: 0.9/0.1 has the best five-fold cross-validation (1.266 vs 1.268 for the grouped curve alone and 1.273 for v3’s 0.8/0.2) and the best blind hold-out (35% of predictions inside ±30%, median error −5.5%, vs 29% and −6.4% for 0.8/0.2); more weight on the second opinion improves walk-forward but costs cross-validation and the blind set, so the curve stays in charge.

Farm plans

Every row carries six steps: a fresh wallet, one category-specific action with its capital and gas, a weekly qualifying action, a hold of 30 to 60 days by the protocol's age, a check of the protocol's own points page before withdrawing, and a farm log. Costs and times are in each step; nothing here is financial advice.
